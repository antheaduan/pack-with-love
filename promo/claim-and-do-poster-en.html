Why let kids raise it themselves? Fundraising is not asking for money. Across generations of civic education it has been understood as training in leadership, communication, entrepreneurship, and citizenship. On the surface a child is raising money; underneath, they face one very real question: "I believe this is worth doing — but why would anyone else support me?" Answering it honestly is the whole curriculum. Done well, fundraising is a complete chain of real work — from discovering a need to reporting back to every supporter.

THE FOUR MOVESWhat do the four moves look like?

  • ① Claim — take an honest inventory (a talent, baking, a bubble-tea resale, a car wash); after a first feasibility check, size the goal — 10 or 20 backpacks alone, 50 / 80 / 100 as a team; a parent confirms it
  • ② Plan — three artifacts: the Ask (one honest paragraph), the Schedule, the Tracking Sheet
  • ③ Do — real stands, real asks, real no's; weekly reconciliation, net not gross; a mid-December RESIZE checkpoint to adjust the goal honestly
  • ④ Report — final accounts + a personal thank-you to every supporter: "Your trust became __ backpacks."

WHAT THEY PRACTICEWhat does your child practice?

  • Commitment — promise in real units, deliver with a season of effort
  • Speaking to strangers — telling their reason to someone they don't know
  • Tracking & reconciling — every dollar traced; cost and profit kept apart
  • Honest revision — resizing mid-season isn't failing; it's deciding again with real data
  • Closing the loop — no supporter learns the outcome from silence
  • A claim = membership — automatic Pack with Love membership, free forever

Where does the money go?

Pack with Love is a youth service club founded by students on both U.S. coasts, with adult advisors holding the guardrails. Every child's claim counts toward the shared $5,300 goal of our first project, the Love & Hope Backpack Project — 1,000 color-it-yourself backpack sets for children across ten villages in Senegal, delivered in person with the March 2027 medical mission. All money moves through adult-managed channels; kids never handle funds. A claim is a commitment to effort, not a debt — any unmet portion is carried by the whole community.

Your child builds real skills; a child far away gets a backpack — win-win.
