Project-Based Math: From Data to Impact

A 24-session, project-based math course where students don't practice on made-up problems — they run a real product business, with real customers, real data, and real stakes.

  • Who

    Grades 6–7 recommended · elementary students welcome with an accompanying parent

  • When

    Sundays, 4:00 PM Pacific · Sept 13, 2026 – Feb 28, 2027 · Final Impact Showcase Mar 7, 2027

  • Format

    24 sessions = 21 classes + 3 real fieldwork experiences (Survey Fieldwork, Sales Events I & II) · ~60 min each

  • Class size

    Capped at 15 students

  • Tuition

    $600 ($25 per session)

The curriculum

What your child will learn

Over 24 sessions, students carry one real project from first question to final presentation — and learn the math that professionals actually use.

Statistical thinking

The six-step data process: pose a statistical question, measure, collect, summarize, analyze, interpret.

Market research

Design a real survey, gather 30–50 real responses, and let evidence — not guessing — drive decisions.

Unit economics

Cost, price, margin, break-even: the arithmetic of every real business, done on their own product.

Data analysis

Turn their own sales records into charts, comparisons, and conclusions about what worked and why.

Decisions under uncertainty

Revise a plan mid-course based on what the data says, not what they hoped.

Public presentation

Close the year by presenting a complete data story to a real audience.

Why this matters now: calculators and AI can compute. What they can't do is judge whether a sample is trustworthy, decide what a percentage means, or choose what to do next. That judgment is what this course builds — and it's built fastest when the numbers are real.
The difference

The numbers are real

In most math programs, the problems are simulations. In this course, the project is real:

Everything the class makes and sells funds the Love & Hope Backpack Project by Pack with Love — delivering color-it-yourself backpack sets to children across ten villages in Senegal. The survey your child designs shapes a real product. The price they set determines real revenue. The final presentation reports real impact — measured in actual backpacks delivered to actual children in March 2027.

Real stakes change how students learn. A wrong answer on a worksheet costs a point. A wrong price at a real sales table costs the project money — and teaches a lesson no worksheet can.

The journey

How the program unfolds

  • 1
    Understanding the Business · 3 sessions
    How a real product project works, from idea to impact.
  • 2
    Market Research & Statistics · 4 sessions
    Design a survey, collect 30–50 real responses, size the opportunity.
  • 3
    Product, Production & Business Math · 6 sessions
    Unit costs, margins, break-even — then the real supplier and cost decision.
  • 4
    Marketing & Sales · 7 sessions
    Real sales events in January; every transaction becomes a data point.
  • 5
    Analyze, Reflect & Impact · 4 sessions
    What does our data say worked? Business review and impact math.
  • 6
    Final Impact Showcase · March 7, 2027
    The full data story, presented publicly.

Enrollment = membership

Enrolling automatically makes your child a member of the Pack with Love Club — the team that carries this project together for the year. No separate application, prerequisite, or prior involvement required. New to all of this? That's expected; the course starts from zero.

Tuition is not tax-deductible

What you are buying is the teaching — twenty-four sessions of it — and a payment made in exchange for something of value is not a charitable gift. Africa Cries Out issues a receipt marked as a fee for services. Please don't file it with your charitable giving.

What your money buys

Backpacks. Every session is taught by volunteers — no part of tuition is anyone's salary, not the instructor's, not an advisor's, not a guest mentor's. Because the teaching is donated, the full $600 goes to the real costs of making and delivering the sets: production, ocean shipping, and customs. Anything beyond what this year's project needs supports the wider work of ACO's Children's Hope & Care program — the children's part of each mission, where the backpacks are handed out.

What counts on the $5,300 bar

Not tuition. The bar exists to show what the community raises together — donations, children's claims, and the class's own sales events — so enrollment money stays out of it.

And the class's sales count at full value, not profit. A child running a bake sale with their own money counts what's left after costs; this class's materials were already paid for out of tuition, which never touched the bar, so subtracting the cost again would remove the same dollars twice.

About the project's partner: Pack with Love delivers this project in partnership with Africa Cries Out, a Christ-centered medical mission nonprofit serving communities in Senegal through annual medical missions, community care, and youth development. Each backpack set includes school supplies and a wordless picture book sharing the Christian message of love. Backpacks are given unconditionally — no child is ever required to attend an activity or share anything to receive one. Families of all backgrounds are welcome in this course; we share this so you can enroll fully informed.
Questions parents ask

FAQ

Does my child need any math background?

No prerequisite coursework. The same real task adjusts to mathematical maturity: foundational learners work with number sense, percentages, and graphs; advanced learners add weighted decision models, profit margin, and forecast error. Grades 6–7 get the best fit; what's required is commitment, not prior coursework.

My child is in elementary school — can they join?

Yes, with an accompanying parent. The parent's role is support, not substitution: the student asks the questions, records the data, and explains the decisions; the parent helps with logistics and safety at fieldwork events. An accompanied student holds one of the 15 seats like everyone else.

Do I need to know anything about Pack with Love or ACO to enroll?

No. The project is fully introduced inside the course. This page and the enrollment conversation contain everything you need to decide.

Is my child required to sell, perform, or fundraise individually?

The class works as a team, with roles matched to each student — research, production, sales, recording, presenting. Every student participates in the full data cycle; no student is graded on dollars raised.

Can my child also make an individual fundraising claim outside class?

Yes — any child, enrolled or not, can claim a portion of the project goal and pursue it with a parent's guidance. The course is the deep version, not the only version. See the three ways in →

Does my tuition show on the $5,300 progress bar?

No, and that's deliberate. Tuition pays for the same real costs the goal covers — production, ocean shipping, customs — but it is held separately. The public bar is there to show what the community raises together: donations, children's claims, and the class's own sales events. If enrollment money went onto it, the bar would move because families signed up rather than because anyone raised anything, and the number would stop meaning what it says.

Is tuition tax-deductible?

No. It buys 24 sessions of teaching, and a payment made in exchange for something of equal value is not a charitable gift. Africa Cries Out receipts it as a fee for services, not as a contribution.

The money the students raise selling is a different matter entirely: it goes to the shared Pack with Love goal and is not returned to families. That is the line — the fee buys the teaching, the sales fund the backpacks.

Attendance, makeup, and refund policies?

Both are published in full on the enrollment page: full refund before the course starts, a prorated refund through the first three sessions, and none from Phase 2 onward — because by then roles are assigned and the project's targets are set with your child counted in. Every missed session comes with a catch-up pack, so a student stays inside the project rather than behind it. Read the full policies →
