This January, a twelve-year-old will stand behind a sales table, watch a stranger pick up a product she helped design, and hold her breath over a number she calculated herself.

Did we price it right? The survey said people would pay this. Was the survey right?

That moment is the course. From Data to Impact hands students one real product business to run, from first survey to final sale. They question real customers, set real prices, count real revenue — and answer for real results. “When will I ever use this?” — the one question no student in this course will ever need to ask.

Why this course, now

Your child has AI.
Now what?

Your child can already ask AI for any answer. So what, exactly, is left to learn?

AI will — in one second

average your survey results. It doesn't know that all 40 responses came from your friends, who were never going to tell you the truth.

AI will — instantly

chart your January sales. It can't decide whether the fix is a lower price, a better sign, or a different table.

It computes flawlessly — and takes responsibility for nothing. The scarce skill of your child's generation isn't getting answers. It's knowing which numbers to trust, what they mean, and what to do next.

That skill has never been teachable from a worksheet, because worksheets have answer keys. It's built the way it's always been built: by making a real call, being really wrong, and getting to try again. This course is 24 sessions of exactly that — with stakes a child can feel and a mission worth being careful for.

Questions parents ask

Before you enroll

Does my child need any math background?

No prerequisite coursework. The same real task adjusts to mathematical maturity: foundational learners work with number sense, percentages, and graphs; advanced learners add weighted decision models, profit margin, and forecast error. Grades 6–7 get the best fit; what's required is commitment, not prior coursework.

My child is in elementary school — can they join?

Yes, with an accompanying parent. The parent's role is support, not substitution: the student asks the questions, records the data, and explains the decisions; the parent helps with logistics and safety at fieldwork events. An accompanied student holds one of the 15 seats like everyone else.

Is my child required to sell or fundraise individually?

The class works as a team, with roles matched to each student — research, production, sales, recording, presenting. Every student participates in the full data cycle; no student is graded on dollars raised.

What if my child has to miss a class?

Let us know ahead when you can. Missed classes aren't refunded or credited individually, but every absent student receives a catch-up packet — the session summary, the class's current data files, and the week's task — so they stay inside the project, not behind it. If a student misses three or more sessions, the coach will talk with the family to right-size the student's role — an adjustment, never a penalty.

What if we can't attend a fieldwork event?

Fieldwork dates are announced at least three weeks in advance — please protect them if you can; they are the heart of the course. A student who truly can't attend is given an alternative real role — pre-event preparation or post-event data analysis — so their contribution stays real, not simulated.

Is tuition refundable?

Before the first session (Sept 13): full refund. Through the end of Phase 1 (the first three sessions, ≈ Sept 27): refund of $720 minus $30 per session attended — Phase 1 is your natural trial window. After Phase 1: no refund, because the class sizes its team claim, survey, and roles on the final roster, and a seat in the 15-student cohort can no longer be refilled mid-project.

Documented medical or relocation circumstances receive prorated credit toward a future program. If we cancel a session, we add a makeup or extend the season; if we ever cancel the course itself, all remaining sessions are refunded in full.

What if delays push things past the March 2027 mission?

Some things are outside anyone's control: customs clearance, tariff policy changes, supplier delays. If any of them keep funds or backpacks from making the March 2027 mission, nothing is lost — every dollar raised supports the 2028 Children's Hope & Care mission instead. The same applies to funds raised beyond the $5,300 goal and to gifts that arrive after the campaign closes. The mission returns every year; love that misses one flight catches the next.

Is any part tax-deductible?

No. Tuition purchases instruction — it is not a charitable donation and is not tax-deductible. (The class's sales proceeds go to the project's shared goal, but those are raised from customers, not from tuition.)
