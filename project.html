The purpose

Complete backpack sets, packed with love

Our founding project supports ACO's Children's Hope & Care program: complete, child-centered backpack sets, packed at the ACO base and shared with children across about ten villages — the children's side of each mission, where kids get Bible stories, songs, games, and crafts in a safe space while their families are seen at the clinic.

Our partner Africa Cries Out is a Christ-centered nonprofit bringing medical care, hope, and compassionate service to communities in Senegal:

30,000+patients served
10+annual missions
100+volunteers a year
Every backpack is a gift, unconditional. No child is ever required to attend an activity or share anything to receive one.

Inside every backpack

Four things that belong together

1

Backpack

A child-friendly backpack with a theme-based coloring design each child can personalize.

2

Coloring Book

A matching book that connects to the backpack's artwork with Bible truths and encouragement.

3

Gospel Wordless Book

A simple, colorful book that shares the good news of Jesus with colors instead of words.

Coloring Set

Optional extras — school supplies, handmade cards, bookmarks — may be added, as long as they never delay the four core items.

The Gospel Wordless Book

The good news of Jesus, told with colors instead of words — so it crosses every language barrier. Each color tells part of the story:

  • Dark · Our sin
  • Red · Jesus' love
  • White · A clean heart
  • Green · Growing with Jesus

How the year unfolds

The year in three acts

Design the gift, raise the means, and deliver it in person. Join for one act — or all three.

Act 1 · Fall

Design & Give

Our student design team creates the gift itself — the color-it-yourself backpack panels and matching coloring books — so the child who receives the backpack finishes the creation the child who designed it began. Idea → design → produce → ship → give.

Act 2 · Fall–Winter

Raise

One shared goal, three ways in: give directly, claim a portion and carry it yourself, or join the Data to Impact course and carry it as a team. Every rung uses the same Four Moves — what changes is depth.

Act 3 · March 2027

Deliver

The loop can close in person: families may join the ACO mission to Senegal — packing sets at the base and placing them, one by one, into the hands they were made for. Optional; registered separately through ACO.

No student travels unprepared

Pre-departure training, led by service trip mentor Dr. Christopher Leo, MD, PhD, FACP — physician and Assistant Professor at Duke University: travel health and hygiene, food and water safety, personal safety, appropriate conduct with local children, and cultural respect. Helping others also means learning how to enter another community with respect.

Done responsibly

One project, one number, one truth

Every dollar from every rung — direct gifts, individual claims, the class's team claim — lands on the same $5,300 shared goal and is recorded the moment it arrives, on the contributor's own progress and the shared total together.

  • Money: all donations are received and managed through ACO's approved financial channels. Students help with planning, design, and communication — never personally collecting, holding, or managing funds.
  • Privacy: children's personal fundraising pages show first names and general grade level only.
  • Giving: every backpack is a gift, unconditional — no child is ever required to attend or share anything to receive one.
  • Beyond the goal: if gifts run past $5,300, nothing is returned and nothing leaves the purpose you gave for — once the 1,000 sets are funded, produced, and shipped, additional gifts support the wider work of ACO's Children's Hope & Care program.

Donations are received by Africa Cries Out, a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit (EIN: 81-3022255), and are tax-deductible to the extent allowed by law. Pack with Love does not itself hold or manage funds.

Before you give

Questions we get asked

What exactly does the $5,300 pay for?

$4,500 covers 1,000 complete backpack sets at $4.50 each — a backpack, a coloring book, a Gospel Wordless Book, and a coloring set. The remaining $800 covers ocean shipping, import duties, and customs clearance, based on ACO's actual shipping history and a confirmed quote. There is one goal and one number: every gift — a direct donation, a child's bake sale, the math class's sales events — lands on that same $5,300.

Is my donation tax-deductible?

Yes. Donations are received by Africa Cries Out, a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit (EIN: 81-3022255), and are tax-deductible to the extent allowed by law. ACO issues the receipt. Pack with Love does not itself hold or manage funds.

Who receives the backpacks, and when?

Children across roughly ten villages in Senegal, through ACO's Children's Hope & Care program — a partner already trusted in those communities. The sets travel with the March 2027 ACO Medical Mission.

And if uncontrollable factors — customs, tariff changes, production or shipping delays — keep funds or backpacks from making that flight, every dollar raised supports the 2028 Children's Hope & Care mission instead. The mission returns every year; love that misses one flight catches the next.

What happens if you raise more than $5,300?

Nothing is returned, and nothing is redirected outside the work you gave toward. The 1,000 sets get funded, produced, and shipped first. Gifts beyond that support the wider work of ACO's Children's Hope & Care program — the children's part of each mission, where these backpacks are handed out alongside Bible stories, songs, games, and crafts. We would rather tell you that now than surprise you with it later.

Does a child have to do anything to receive a backpack?

No. Every backpack is a gift, unconditional. No child is ever required to attend an activity, believe anything, or share anything to receive one. The same holds on our side: a child who claims part of the goal commits to effort, not a debt — an oversized claim gets resized at a normal checkpoint, not treated as a failure.

Still have a question? Ask us directly — a real person answers.
