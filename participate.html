Why we do it this way

Fundraising is not asking for money.
It is a complete real-world project.

Across generations of civic education, fundraising has been understood as training in leadership, communication, entrepreneurship, and citizenship. On the surface, a child is raising money. Underneath, they come face to face with one very real question:

“I believe this is worth doing — but why would anyone else support me?”

Answering that question honestly is the whole curriculum. Done well, fundraising is a complete chain of real work — from discovering a need all the way to reporting back to every supporter.

The world is changing quickly

Three questions AI cannot answer for anyone

A poster, a website, a budget spreadsheet, a fundraising letter — work that once took a team, AI now helps produce in minutes. So the gap between people is no longer “who can make it?” It is becoming:

Who can make people care?

AI can

write the fundraising email.

It cannot

make the reader care.

Who can earn trust?

AI can

find a hundred potential donors.

It cannot

make one of them believe “this young person is serious, and I want to support them.”

Who can organize people?

AI can

generate a beautiful presentation.

It cannot

recruit a friend, coordinate a team, or stand in front of a room and take responsibility for “why does this matter?”

Knowledge → Skill → Produce something ⟶ Problem → Judgment → Communication → Mobilization → Execution

Fundraising strings all five together — a durably human skill set: abilities that appreciate, not depreciate, as AI advances.

Claim Plan Do Report

The same four moves whether you run a lemonade stand or a year-long data project — what changes is depth. Each capability above gets practiced somewhere in these four.

The project clock

One year, one calendar

Every family sees the same calendar. All dates are approximate — the real project comes first.

≈ Mid–late Sept 2026

Move 1 — Claim

Claims registered & parent-confirmed.

≈ Late Sept – mid Oct 2026

Move 2 — Plan

The ask, the schedule, the tracking sheet.

≈ Mid Oct 2026 – Feb 28, 2027

Move 3 — Do

The full season — including Sales Events I & II in January — with weekly reconciliation.

≈ Mid Dec 2026

RESIZE checkpoint

Claims resized or confirmed before the holiday-season push. Resizing is smart, not failing — released portions return to the shared pool.

≈ Nov – Dec 2026

Production order placed

Funded by the bridge advance, per the supplier's confirmed lead time; ocean shipping takes 5–6 weeks.

≈ March 1, 2027

Funds close

All money in; the $5,300 bar is final.

≈ March 1–6, 2027

Move 4 — Report

Final accounts, thank-yous, impact statements.

March 7, 2027

Final Impact Showcase

“Your trust became ___ backpacks.” — the final number, presented.

Two clocks, one bridge

Production never waits on the progress bar

The production order is guaranteed by a bridge advance from the founding family, and donations repay the bridge as they arrive. If the season ends short, the gap becomes the founding family's own gift — never a child's burden.

No child owes the gap. A claim is a commitment to effort, not a debt.

How to start a claim

Free for any child, parent-guided, and sized by you. Five steps from idea to a live page.

1Take inventory

What can you perform, make, resell, or offer — and who do you already know?

2Size it

In real units: ten backpacks alone, twenty-five with two friends. Effort, not debt.

3Parent registers

A parent sets up the claim from their own ACO account.

4Page goes live

ACO reads it once, then your page — with its own bar and donation link — is public.

5Walk the Moves

Plan, do, resize at the midpoint, and report back to every supporter.
